Tutorial 3 - In-class exercise answers (Vir Patel)

1. Map the untrimmed files to the genome. How do the alignments compare?
   Overall alignment rates (bowtie2), samples SRR6805880 ... SRR6805885:
     untrimmed (TGCAG left on):  22.00, 21.30, 22.30, 21.30, 21.20, 23.10 %   (mean ~21.9%)
     trimmed (TGCAG removed):    21.80, 21.80, 22.80, 20.80, 21.10, 23.40 %   (mean ~22.0%)
   The alignments are almost identical; removing the 5-nt TGCAG changed individual samples by less than
   about 1 percentage point (some up, some down). Both rates are low (~20-23%) because the genome and reads
   were subsampled for this exercise. Bowtie2 soft-clips (local mode) mismatching read ends, which is why
   leaving the TGCAG on does not hurt the alignment much.

2. Trim the sequences to 70 bp (like Xuereb et al. 2018) and map them. How do they compare to the first trimmed reads?
   Command: cutadapt -g TGCAG -l 70 (adapter removed, then reads shortened to 70 bp); output files named *.tiny_trimmed70.fastq.
   Overall alignment rates: 21.10, 21.20, 22.60, 20.50, 20.50, 22.60 %   (mean ~21.4%)
   Compared with the first trimmed reads (mean ~22.0%), the 70 bp reads align slightly less well (about 0.2-1.0
   points lower in every sample except SRR6805881, which is unchanged within 0.6). Shorter reads carry less
   information, so fewer of them can be placed uniquely/confidently. The difference is small.

3. Change the angsd SNP p-value. How many more/fewer SNPs?
   The original run (SNP_pval 1e-2) recovered 9 SNPs (out of 71,450 sites analyzed).
   Two (three) other p-values I tried:
     SNP_pval 1e-1 (less strict): 54 SNPs
     SNP_pval 1e-3 (stricter):     0 SNPs
     SNP_pval 1e-6 (even stricter): 0 SNPs
   Raising the p-value threshold (less strict) recovers many more SNPs (54 vs 9); lowering it (more strict)
   removes all of them here, because with only 6 low-coverage individuals there is little statistical power.
